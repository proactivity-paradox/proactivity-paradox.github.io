About the workshop

When does helping become overstepping?

The same initiative that makes proactive AI useful can also short-circuit human reflection, judgment, and agency. This is the proactivity paradox.

Recent advances in generative AI are enabling systems to move beyond responding to explicit user requests toward anticipating needs and taking initiative on users’ behalf. AI systems increasingly determine when to intervene, what to suggest, and which actions to take without an explicit request.

Greater AI initiative can make assistance more useful while simultaneously reducing opportunities for people to deliberate, decide, and remain meaningfully involved. This workshop examines how proactive systems can act effectively while supporting people’s ability to exercise agency, shape decisions, and retain meaningful control in human–AI interactions.

Connecting perspectives from cognitive science, HCI, social science, and AI, we will explore not only how to design proactive agents, but when and under what conditions their initiative is appropriate. Interdisciplinary discussions and collaborative activities will connect how humans exercise initiative with how proactive AI systems are designed and evaluated.

Guiding questions

What should proactivity preserve?

Who shapes the decision?

What changes over time?

How might sustained interaction with proactive AI reshape human cognitive abilities?
